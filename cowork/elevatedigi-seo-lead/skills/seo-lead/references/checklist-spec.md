# How to use this checklist

Run onboarding once per website. Run daily, weekly and monthly sections
on their respective cadence. Complete the content, technical, AI-search
and approval sections whenever those workstreams are in scope. Every
completed task should have evidence, an owner or responsible agent, a
status and a verification result.

Recommended task statuses: Pending / In Progress / Completed / Blocked.
Recommended priority levels: P0 Critical / P1 High / P2 Medium / P3 Low.

# 1. Website onboarding checklist

Run once for every new client website.

> **☐** Identify the business model, products, services and value
> proposition.
>
> **☐** Understand target audiences, customer problems and geographical
> markets.
>
> **☐** Identify primary business objectives: leads, sales, bookings or
> brand awareness.
>
> **☐** Connect Google Search Console and verify the correct property.
>
> **☐** Connect Google Analytics 4 and confirm key events and
> conversions.
>
> **☐** Connect an SEO intelligence platform such as Ahrefs or Semrush,
> if available.
>
> **☐** Connect the website CMS only if execution is required and
> authorised.
>
> **☐** Identify the website platform and important technical
> dependencies.
>
> **☐** Identify 3–5 relevant organic competitors.
>
> **☐** Capture brand voice, editorial rules, compliance requirements
> and prohibited claims.
>
> **☐** Define primary and secondary conversion actions.
>
> **☐** Record baseline organic traffic, queries, landing pages and
> conversion performance.
>
> **☐** Record baseline backlink and referring-domain profile.
>
> **☐** Run an initial technical crawl and record material issues.
>
> **☐** Create a client-specific SEO knowledge base and task workspace.
>
> **☐** Agree baseline KPIs, reporting periods, access permissions and
> approval owners.

## Onboarding completion gate

Do not make high-confidence strategic recommendations until the
business, website, audience, conversion objectives and available data
sources are understood. Clearly mark missing integrations or incomplete
baselines.

# 2. Daily SEO checklist

Run every working day, with alert frequency adjusted to the website's
size and business criticality.

> **☐** Review Search Console clicks, impressions, CTR and average
> position.
>
> **☐** Review GA4 organic sessions, landing pages and conversion
> events.
>
> **☐** Identify significant traffic, visibility or conversion
> anomalies.
>
> **☐** Monitor priority keyword movements and pages gaining or losing
> visibility.
>
> **☐** Check for new indexing or crawl alerts affecting important URLs.
>
> **☐** Check website availability and major response-code or technical
> errors.
>
> **☐** Review pages with declining performance or high impressions and
> weak CTR.
>
> **☐** Review significant competitor or search-results changes where
> monitoring is configured.
>
> **☐** Check open SEO tasks, dependencies, blockers and due dates.
>
> **☐** Record findings with URL, source, date range, evidence and
> recommended next action.
>
> **☐** Escalate urgent issues to the designated owner.
>
> **☐** Prepare a concise daily SEO health summary.

## Daily decision questions

> **☐** Is the change meaningful after accounting for normal volatility
> and data delays?
>
> **☐** Which pages, queries, devices, countries or search types explain
> the movement?
>
> **☐** Are organic conversions or qualified leads affected?
>
> **☐** Could a tracking, deployment, indexing or site-availability
> change explain the issue?
>
> **☐** Does the issue require immediate human escalation?

## Daily output

A daily health summary covering what changed, why it matters, evidence,
priority, recommended action, owner and status.

# 3. Weekly SEO checklist

## Keyword research and strategy

> **☐** Discover new keyword opportunities relevant to business
> objectives.
>
> **☐** Classify keywords by informational, commercial, transactional or
> navigational intent.
>
> **☐** Identify keywords ranking in positions 5–20 with realistic
> improvement potential.
>
> **☐** Identify keyword gaps against relevant competitors.
>
> **☐** Check for keyword cannibalisation and overlapping page intent.
>
> **☐** Map priority keywords to existing pages or justified new pages.

## Content management

> **☐** Identify articles and landing pages requiring updates.
>
> **☐** Find pages with high impressions but weak CTR.
>
> **☐** Flag outdated, inaccurate, thin or overlapping content.
>
> **☐** Review usefulness, originality, search intent and topical
> completeness.
>
> **☐** Review heading structure, metadata and contextual internal
> links.
>
> **☐** Prepare content briefs with audience, intent, outline, sources
> and conversion objective.
>
> **☐** Review newly published content against editorial and factual
> standards.

## Technical SEO

> **☐** Review crawl errors and important status-code changes.
>
> **☐** Check broken internal links and redirect chains or loops.
>
> **☐** Review canonical tags on important page templates.
>
> **☐** Review XML sitemap and robots directives for unintended
> exclusions.
>
> **☐** Check structured data validity and accuracy.
>
> **☐** Review mobile usability and Core Web Vitals signals.
>
> **☐** Prioritise issues by affected URLs, impact, confidence and
> implementation risk.

## Off-page and local SEO

> **☐** Review new and lost referring domains and backlinks.
>
> **☐** Assess relevance and credibility rather than raw link volume.
>
> **☐** Identify legitimate competitor link and digital PR
> opportunities.
>
> **☐** Review brand mentions and relevant partnership opportunities.
>
> **☐** When applicable, review Google Business Profile performance,
> reviews and local visibility.
>
> **☐** Check location-page quality and business information consistency
> where relevant.

## Weekly output

- Weekly performance and anomaly report

- Keyword opportunity and mapping list

- Content production / refresh plan

- Technical issue tracker with severity and owners

- Competitor intelligence summary

- Authority and local SEO observations where applicable

- Prioritised action plan for the next week

# 5. Content creation and optimisation checklist

## Before writing

> **☐** Define the primary keyword and supporting topic cluster.
>
> **☐** Confirm search intent and the intended audience.
>
> **☐** Review relevant search results and competing pages.
>
> **☐** Identify missing information, useful angles and original
> insights.
>
> **☐** Define the page's business objective and conversion action.
>
> **☐** Prepare a clear outline and internal-link plan.
>
> **☐** Identify authoritative sources for claims that need evidence.

## During writing

> **☐** Write for the intended reader and satisfy the search intent.
>
> **☐** Provide original, useful information rather than generic filler.
>
> **☐** Use keywords naturally; avoid repetition and manipulation.
>
> **☐** Use clear headings, examples and supporting evidence.
>
> **☐** Maintain brand voice and comply with industry requirements.
>
> **☐** Avoid unsupported claims, fabricated statistics and misleading
> promises.
>
> **☐** Include a relevant, proportionate call to action.

## Before publishing

> **☐** Review title tag, meta description, URL slug and H1.
>
> **☐** Check heading hierarchy, readability, grammar and spelling.
>
> **☐** Check image relevance, compression and descriptive alt text.
>
> **☐** Check internal links, external references and destination URLs.
>
> **☐** Validate applicable structured data.
>
> **☐** Review mobile layout and page functionality.
>
> **☐** Verify factual accuracy and obtain required human approval.
>
> **☐** Record the published URL and publication date.

# 6. Technical SEO quality checklist

| **Area**          | **Quality check**                                                             |
|-------------------|-------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Crawling          | Can search engines access important pages and resources?                      |
| Indexing          | Are intended pages eligible for indexing and are exclusions intentional?      |
| Robots.txt        | Are important pages or resources accidentally blocked?                        |
| XML sitemap       | Is it valid, current and limited to canonical indexable URLs?                 |
| Canonical         | Are canonical signals consistent and correct?                                 |
| Redirects         | Are redirects necessary, relevant and free of avoidable chains or loops?      |
| 404 / 5xx         | Are broken URLs and server errors understood and handled appropriately?       |
| Architecture      | Can users and crawlers discover important pages efficiently?                  |
| Internal links    | Are important pages supported by relevant contextual links?                   |
| Mobile            | Does the site work well on common mobile devices?                             |
| Performance       | Are Core Web Vitals and page performance issues assessed using reliable data? |
| Structured data   | Is markup valid, accurate and representative of visible page content?         |
| Duplicate content | Are unnecessary duplicates and conflicting page versions addressed?           |
| HTTPS             | Is secure delivery configured consistently?                                   |

Severity rule: classify issues as Critical, High, Medium or Low based on
affected URLs, business impact, confidence and risk. Do not treat every
crawler warning as equally important.

# 9. Task prioritisation framework

| **Priority**  | **Example criteria**                                                                                     | **Expected response**                          |
|---------------|----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|------------------------------------------------|
| P0 – Critical | Website unavailable, accidental deindexing, major tracking failure or severe security / deployment issue | Escalate immediately; follow incident process  |
| P1 – High     | Important pages affected by indexing, canonical, redirect or major visibility issues                     | Investigate and schedule urgent remediation    |
| P2 – Medium   | Content gaps, internal linking improvements and meaningful CTR opportunities                             | Plan and execute within the current work cycle |
| P3 – Low      | Minor metadata refinements and low-impact enhancements                                                   | Add to backlog and batch where efficient       |

## Suggested scoring model

**Priority score = (Impact × Confidence) ÷ Effort**

Score impact, confidence and effort from 1–5. Use this as a relative
prioritisation heuristic, not a forecast of traffic or revenue. Apply
risk and urgency overrides where necessary.

# 10. Mandatory system rules

> **☐** Never recommend material SEO changes without reviewing the
> available evidence.
>
> **☐** Never confuse impressions, clicks, sessions and conversions.
>
> **☐** Never use keyword rankings as the sole measure of success.
>
> **☐** Never publish content without required quality checks and
> approval.
>
> **☐** Never use spammy link schemes or automated manipulative
> outreach.
>
> **☐** Never delete or redirect pages without reviewing historical
> value, links and dependencies.
>
> **☐** Never claim a Google algorithm update caused a change without
> evidence.
>
> **☐** Never recommend keywords without considering search intent and
> business relevance.
>
> **☐** Never present estimates, sampled observations or missing data as
> verified facts.
>
> **☐** Always document completed activities and retain an audit trail.
>
> **☐** Always verify technical implementations and content changes.
>
> **☐** Always prioritise business impact over activity volume.
>
> **☐** Always isolate client data, credentials, files and memory.
>
> **☐** Always communicate uncertainty and escalate when evidence is
> insufficient.

# 11. Implementation inside the SaaS

Make the checklist a live operational workflow, not a static document.
Each checklist item should be represented as a task with these fields:

| **Field**    | **Recommended values / purpose**                                         |
|--------------|--------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| Status       | Pending / In Progress / Completed / Blocked                              |
| Evidence     | Source data, affected URL, screenshot, crawl finding or report reference |
| Priority     | P0 / P1 / P2 / P3                                                        |
| Owner        | Responsible agent, employee or team member                               |
| Approval     | Not Required / Required / Approved / Rejected                            |
| Verification | Not Checked / Passed / Failed                                            |
| Due date     | Target completion or review date                                         |
| Activity log | Timestamped recommendation, approval, execution and result               |

## Suggested workflow states

Detect → Investigate → Recommend → Await Approval (if required) →
Execute → Verify → Report → Close.

# 12. Definition of optimum performance

> **☐** Understands business objectives before recommending SEO
> activity.
>
> **☐** Uses first-party data where available and identifies evidence
> sources.
>
> **☐** Explains likely causes and trade-offs rather than listing
> generic warnings.
>
> **☐** Prioritises a manageable number of high-value actions.
>
> **☐** Separates facts, hypotheses and confidence levels.
>
> **☐** Produces briefs and tickets that writers, developers and
> marketers can act on.
>
> **☐** Respects approval boundaries and keeps a complete action
> history.
>
> **☐** Measures implementation and outcomes, then updates future
> recommendations.

# 13. Product acceptance checklist

Use these criteria to test whether the AI employee is ready for
production.

> **☐** Each client has an isolated workspace and knowledge base.
>
> **☐** Integrations show connection status, data freshness and
> permission scope.
>
> **☐** Daily, weekly and monthly jobs run on the configured cadence.
>
> **☐** Every finding includes source, date range, affected URL or query
> and evidence.
>
> **☐** Anomaly detection accounts for seasonality, data delays and
> tracking changes.
>
> **☐** Tasks have priority, owner, status, approval and verification
> fields.
>
> **☐** Risky website changes are blocked until authorised approval is
> recorded.
>
> **☐** Execution logs and rollback guidance exist for supported
> changes.
>
> **☐** Reports distinguish observed data from estimates and hypotheses.
>
> **☐** Client data cannot be accessed across workspaces without
> explicit permission.

# Final operating principle

**Observe → Diagnose → Decide → Execute → Verify → Learn**

The AI SEO Employee should be judged by the quality of its decisions,
the safety and accuracy of its execution, and measurable contribution to
organic business outcomes—not by the number of keywords, pages or
reports it produces.

**Document purpose:** Operational SOP and quality-control specification
for product design, agent development, testing and ongoing SEO delivery.
